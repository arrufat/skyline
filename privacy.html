Skyline privacy policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

I made Skyline because I wanted a weather app that's clear to read and free. It has no server of its own. Your places and settings stay on your device. The only requests it makes are the ones listed here.

What the app sends

To show the weather, your phone or watch asks these free services directly. Like any website, each one sees your IP address and what was asked:

  • Open-Meteo gets the coordinates of your places, for the forecast and the rain map. It also gets what you type when you search for a place.
  • National weather services give the official warnings, such as MeteoAlarm in Europe or the US National Weather Service. Some get a place's coordinates.
  • Map tiles come from OpenFreeMap for the streets, and from SSEC RealEarth (University of Wisconsin–Madison) for the clouds.
  • Your device's geocoder, usually Google's, names a place on the map or where you are.

That list is everything the app sends, and none of it is sold or shared.

Location

Skyline reads your device's location only if you allow it, to show the weather where you are. It reads it in the background only if you allow it all the time. That is for rain alerts and the weather notification. Your places and recent forecasts stay on your device. They are deleted when you uninstall the app.

In a browser

The app at skyline.arrufat.io/app asks the same services from your browser, except your device's geocoder. Its files come from GitHub Pages, which sees your IP address like any web host. Your places and choices are kept in your browser's storage for this site. Clearing it removes them. Your location is read only if you ask for it and your browser allows it.

Changes
