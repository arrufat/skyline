Skyline privacy policy

Last updated: 12 November 2026

Skyline is a weather app for Android phones and Wear OS watches. It has no accounts, no ads, and no analytics, and it never sends your data to a server of its own: Skyline has none.

Location

Skyline uses a place's coordinates to fetch its weather. That place is either one you added or, if you choose Use device location, where your phone or watch is. Your device's location is read only when you allow it, and in the background only if you allow location all the time, for rain alerts and the weather notification. Your places and the last forecasts are kept on your device, and are removed when you uninstall the app or clear its data.

What is sent, and to whom

To show the weather, the app asks these services directly from your device. Like any website, each sees your device's IP address and what it asked for:

  • Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com): the coordinates of each place, for its forecast, air quality, past days, and rain map; and the words you type when searching for a place.
  • National weather services, for official warnings: some, such as the US National Weather Service, are asked with a place's coordinates; others, such as MeteoAlarm in Europe, send their whole country's or region's warnings, which the app sorts on your device.
  • Map tiles, for the rain map and the globe: OpenFreeMap for the streets, Open-Meteo for the rain, and SSEC RealEarth (University of Wisconsin–Madison) for the clouds. Each sees which part of the map is shown.
  • Your device's geocoder, to name a place you tap on the map or your device's location. On most Android devices this is a Google service, under Google's privacy policy.

Skyline sends none of this anywhere else, and none of it is sold or shared for advertising.

Notifications

Rain alerts and the weather notification are worked out on your device from the forecasts above. They are off until you turn them on.

Children

Skyline is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone.
